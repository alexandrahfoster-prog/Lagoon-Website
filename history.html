140 Years of Fun

The History of Lagoon

From a lakeside bathing resort to one of the last family-owned amusement parks in America.

1886 — A resort on the Great Salt Lake

Lagoon's roots go back to July 1886, when a bathing resort called Lake Park opened on the shores of the Great Salt Lake, a few miles west of Farmington. As the lake gradually receded, the resort found itself stranded further from the water, and by 1896 the operation had relocated to its current site and reopened under a new name: Lagoon.

That makes Lagoon the 25th oldest amusement park in the world still operating on its original site, and the 15th oldest in the United States — and the only one in Utah.

The Freed family era

By the mid-1940s, Lagoon was struggling and at risk of closing for good. Two local businessmen convinced the park's owners to lease it to a newly formed company instead of shutting it down, and one of those men's descendants — the Freed family — has been central to Lagoon ever since. The family eventually purchased the park outright, and Lagoon remains one of only a handful of major U.S. amusement parks never bought out by a corporate chain.

Rebuilding, and the big band years

A major fire in the early 1950s destroyed a significant part of the park, and it was rebuilt from the ground up. Around the same era, Lagoon's stage became a stop for some of the biggest names in American music — big band leaders and jazz legends played here at a time when amusement parks doubled as regional concert venues.

Pioneer Village

Tucked into the park is a preserved collection of 19th-century Utah buildings and artifacts known as Pioneer Village. The oldest structure still standing in its original location anywhere on Lagoon's property is a small jail building there dating to 1895 — and the oldest structure in the entire park is a log cabin originally built in Ogden in 1851, predating Lagoon itself by decades. Lagoon today, by the numbers

52 rides, 11 coasters

Spanning over a century of amusement park design.

Oldest rides

The Carousel (1918), the wooden Roller Coaster (1921), and Flying Aces (1941) — one of only 13 original Flying Scooter rides left operating anywhere in the world.

Newest rides

Nutcracker (2026), Time Tinker & Steamworx (2025), and Primordial (2023).

One-of-a-kind coasters

Wicked, BomBora, Cannibal, and Primordial are all unique, one-off ride models — no other park in the world has an identical version.

60 acres

The park itself covers about 60 acres of a roughly 160-acre property — double its original size.

Rides with a past life

Several rides didn't start at Lagoon — the Carousel came from California (built ~1913), and Jet Star II began life at the 1974 World's Fair in Spokane before moving here in 1976.
