The History of Lagoon

From a lakeside bathing resort to one of the last family-owned amusement parks in America.

1886 — A resort on the Great Salt Lake

Lagoon's roots go back to July 1886, when a bathing resort called Lake Park opened on the shores of the Great Salt Lake. As the lake receded, the resort relocated to its current site and reopened in 1896 under a new name: Lagoon.

That makes Lagoon the 25th oldest amusement park in the world still operating on its original site, and the 15th oldest in the United States — the only one in Utah.

The Freed family era

By the mid-1940s, Lagoon was struggling and at risk of closing. Two local businessmen convinced the owners to lease it to a newly formed company instead — and one man's descendants, the Freed family, have been central to Lagoon ever since. The family eventually purchased the park outright, and it remains one of only a handful of major U.S. amusement parks never bought out by a corporate chain.

Rebuilding, and the big band years

A major fire in the early 1950s destroyed a significant part of the park, and it was rebuilt. Around the same era, Lagoon's stage became a stop for some of the biggest names in American big band and jazz music.

Pioneer Village

A preserved collection of 19th-century Utah buildings and artifacts. The oldest structure still standing in its original location on Lagoon's property is a small jail dating to 1895 — and the oldest structure in the entire park is a log cabin originally built in Ogden in 1851. Lagoon today, by the numbers

52 rides, 11 coasters

Spanning over a century of amusement park design.

Oldest rides

The Carousel (1918), the wooden Roller Coaster (1921), and Flying Aces (1941) — one of only 13 original Flying Scooters left anywhere in the world.

Newest rides

Nutcracker (2026), Time Tinker & Steamworx (2025), and Primordial (2023).

One-of-a-kind coasters

Wicked, BomBora, Cannibal, and Primordial are all unique, one-off ride models.

60 acres

The park covers about 60 acres of a roughly 160-acre property.

Rides with a past life

The Carousel came from California (~1913); Jet Star II began at the 1974 World's Fair in Spokane before moving here in 1976.
