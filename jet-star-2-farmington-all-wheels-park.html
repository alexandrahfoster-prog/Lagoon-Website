Jet Star 2's Second Act: Lagoon's Retired Coaster Heads to Farmington's All-Wheels Park

Lagoon said goodbye to its 49-year-old Schwarzkopf coaster last fall. But Jet Star 2 isn't leaving Farmington. Pieces of it, including one of its cars, are headed to the city's new All-Wheels Park.

By Sasha FosterSeptember 23, 20266 min read

The quick version

  • Retired: Jet Star 2 gave its last rides on November 9, 2025, after 49 seasons at Lagoon.
  • What's next: Farmington City got salvaged pieces of the ride, including one of its cars, for its new All-Wheels Park.
  • Where: The old Glovers Park site, just south of Canyon Creek Elementary.
  • When: The city plans to start construction in 2027.

If you grew up in Utah, there's a good chance Jet Star 2 was one of your first "real" roller coasters. It didn't go upside down, and it wasn't the tallest ride in the park. But that slow electric spiral up to the top, followed by tight, whippy turns in a little single car, made it a North Midway favorite for almost half a century.

When Lagoon announced it was retiring the ride, a lot of us assumed that was the end of the story. It turns out Jet Star 2 is getting a second act, just down the road.

A bittersweet goodbye

Lagoon announced on October 22, 2025, that Jet Star 2 would close at the end of the season. Its final day was November 9, 2025, and crews took the ride down after the park closed for the year.

"While it is still a popular attraction, the time has come to make the bittersweet decision to retire it at the end of the 2025 season." Adam Leishman, Lagoon spokesman, to the Deseret News (October 2025)

Lagoon also promised fans that it was planning "a worthy successor." [Update here if Lagoon has since announced what's going in Jet Star 2's old spot on the North Midway.]

From a world's fair to Farmington

Jet Star 2 didn't start out at Lagoon. It was built for Expo '74, the world's fair in Spokane, Washington, where it opened on May 4, 1974. After the fair's six-month run, the ride moved to Lagoon and was re-erected in Farmington for the 1976 season, advertising it as "the only one of its kind in North America."

The ride was made by Schwarzkopf, the legendary German coaster builder, and designed by the engineering firm Ingenieur Büro Stengel. Fun fact: the "2" doesn't mean it was Lagoon's second Jet Star. It was the second model in Schwarzkopf's Jet Star series. The first Jet Star opened in Austria in 1968.

Lagoon kept it running for decades with some serious upkeep:

  • 1991: Sections of track replaced.
  • 1998: A new computerized control system installed.
  • 2004–2005: The ride was completely taken apart and refurbished, and repainted with yellow track and red supports (the reverse of its original colors).
  • 2012: A dual loading platform added to move lines faster.

Its second act: Farmington's All-Wheels Park

Farmington's original skate park, built in 1994, has to come out to make room for the I-15 expansion. Instead of just replacing it, the city is building something bigger: an All-Wheels Park for skateboards, bikes, rollerblades and scooters, at the old Glovers Park site just south of Canyon Creek Elementary.

According to the city's conceptual plans, the park will include:

  • A skate park with rails, stairs, ramps and "plenty of places to grind"
  • Bike jump features and ramps
  • A pump track for experienced riders, plus a junior pump track for beginners
  • A parking lot and an observation deck
  • Salvaged pieces of Jet Star 2
"…lucky enough to obtain salvaged portions of Lagoon's Jet Star amusement park ride, which we plan to incorporate into the design and features." Farmington City, All-Wheels Park conceptual plans

Farmington's September newsletter added that the pieces include one of the ride's cars. The city hasn't said exactly how the parts will be used yet. [Add anything you learn about the design.]

The city was also taking suggestions for the park's name. Local coaster fans have floated "Jet Star Park" (and, of course, "Jet Star 3"). [Update if the city announces a name.]

When will it open? Farmington says construction is planned to begin in 2027. There's no opening date yet.

Frequently asked questions

When did Jet Star 2 close at Lagoon?

Its last day was November 9, 2025, the final day of Lagoon's 2025 season. It ran at Lagoon for 49 seasons, starting in 1976.

Why was Jet Star 2 retired?

Lagoon said the ride had reached the end of its service life. It was still popular, but after nearly 50 years (and 52 counting its time at Expo '74), the park decided it was time.

Why is it called Jet Star "2"?

It was the second model in manufacturer Schwarzkopf's Jet Star line, not Lagoon's second Jet Star.

Where is Jet Star 2 going?

Farmington City got salvaged pieces of the ride, including one of its cars, for its new All-Wheels Park at the old Glovers Park site, south of Canyon Creek Elementary.

When will the All-Wheels Park open?

Farmington plans to start construction in 2027. It hasn't announced an opening date.
