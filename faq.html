Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions every first-timer asks, answered in one place.

Is Lagoon worth it if I've been to Disneyland?

They're genuinely different experiences. Lagoon is smaller and less elaborately themed, but it's also far cheaper per visit, lets you bring your own food, and has a real vintage/local character — the 1921 wooden coaster and 1918 carousel aren't reproductions, they're the real thing. See our full Lagoon vs. Disneyland breakdown.


How long do we need — is one day enough?

One day covers the rides comfortably for most families. If you're also doing Lagoon-A-Beach, plan to arrive at opening to fit both in.


What should we absolutely not skip?

Primordial and Cannibal for thrill-seekers, the 1921 Roller Coaster for the history, and Pioneer Village for a slower-paced break. Full breakdown on the rides page.


Can we really bring our own food?

Yes — Lagoon has allowed outside food and drinks throughout nearly its entire history, unlike most major U.S. parks. See the food guide for what to bring.


How much does parking cost?

Standard parking is $20, preferred (closer) is $35, and RV/oversized is $30. Full pricing on the tickets & pricing page.


Are tickets priced by age or height?

By height, not age — see the pricing page for current thresholds.


Is Lagoon a good spot for a family reunion?

Yes — Lagoon hosts a large number of reunions and group events every season, with discounted group rates and a reservable terrace. Details on the pricing page.


What's the deal with Frightmares?

Lagoon's Halloween event runs weekends (and some weekdays) from September into early November, with haunted houses, a separate immersive Séance show, and non-scary kids' attractions. Full details on the Frightmares page.


Can we stay overnight nearby?

Yes — there's an on-site RV park and campground, plus several hotels within a few miles in Farmington. See RV & Campground and Where to Stay.
