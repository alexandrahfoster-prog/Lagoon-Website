Hi, welcome to my Lagoon blog!

My family and I have called Farmington, Utah home for almost 15 years, and one of our favorite things about it is summer days spent at Lagoon — whether that's a quick couple of hours squeezed between other plans, or a full day meeting up with friends.

Some days that means floating around the lazy river at Lagoon-A-Beach, or playing cards on beach chairs while the kids hit every waterslide. Other days it's repeated rounds on our favorite rides — Cannibal, The Rocket, Tilt-A-Whirl (a controversial favorite, I know), Sky Coaster, and Rattlesnake Rapids. It's always a good time.

As the park has grown, I've gotten a little frustrated by how few good resources exist for planning a Lagoon trip — so I decided to build my own. Here you'll find an updated, comprehensive map of every ride, plus a second one covering food, restrooms, and other attractions, along with tips on when to go and what to bring.

My husband grew up in Southern California going to Disneyland almost every week, so between the two of us, we've got plenty of opinions on how the parks compare, whether a season pass is worth it, and what to actually do once you're there.

Join us on our Lagoon adventure, and let us know if there's anything we missed or more information you'd like to see!

Lagoon, at a glance

Founded 1886

Opened as the Lake Park Bathing Resort on the shores of the Great Salt Lake, then relocated and reopened as "Lagoon" in 1896 — the 25th oldest amusement park in the world still on its original form.

Family-owned

The Freed family has run Lagoon since the 1940s — one of the few major U.S. amusement parks never bought out by a corporate chain.

52 rides, 11 coasters

Including a full water park, the largest Kiddieland of any park in the country, and Pioneer Village.

Bring your own food

Lagoon has allowed outside food and drinks throughout nearly its entire history — a real budget-saver.

Open late March–October

Plus Frightmares, Lagoon's Halloween event, running weekends into early November.

18 miles from Salt Lake City

Located in Farmington, right off I-15 — an easy day trip from most of the Wasatch Front.
